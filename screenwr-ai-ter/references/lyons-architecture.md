# Lyons Architecture — Moral Component and Narrative Engine

*From Jeff Lyons, Rapid Story Development (Routledge, 2020)*

Use this reference when a story has no center, the middle is mushy, or the protagonist feels passive. The Lyons architecture locates the engine that powers a story's middle: the Moral Component.

---

## The Moral Component: The Story's Engine

Every story that WORKS has a moral component running its middle. Without it, you have a situation, not a story. Three interlocking elements:

### 1. Moral Blind Spot

An unconscious core belief that twists the protagonist's moral compass and poisons all external relationships.

- The protagonist does NOT know they have it
- It is rooted in the character's Enneagram type (core fear + core poison)
- It must be expressible in ONE sentence ("They believe that...")
- If you can't say it in one sentence, it's not clear enough

**Discovery questions:**
- "What would they have to believe about other people to justify acting badly?"
- "What would they have to believe about themselves to justify that worldview?"

### 2. Immoral Effect

The blind spot in action — how the protagonist's skewed belief produces behavior that HURTS OTHER PEOPLE on the page.

- This is NOT internal angst or private suffering
- It must be VISIBLE on screen: damage to relationships, choices that cost others
- Without an immoral effect, there is no moral arc
- This is what the audience watches; the blind spot is what they understand

### 3. Dynamic Moral Tension

The engine that keeps the story running. Every serious choice stems from the blind spot and immoral effect.

- Creates escalating stakes in the middle
- Forces the protagonist into increasingly costly decisions
- The tension resolves (or fails to resolve) at the climax

## The Narrative Engine Middle: Seven-Step Architecture

The Lyons method builds the middle through seven steps. When diagnosing a mushy middle, locate which step is missing or weak.

| Step | What It Does | Diagnostic Signal When Missing |
|------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| 1. Moral Component | Establishes the wound driving all behavior | Protagonist has no reason to be wrong about anything |
| 2. Protagonist Enneagram Style | Grounds psychology in a specific type | Character feels generic or inconsistent |
| 3. Evolution/De-Evolution | Maps the arc extremes | Transformation feels arbitrary or unearned |
| 4. Hot Buttons (Pinches/Crunches) | Specifies what triggers the protagonist | Middle has no escalation, antagonist feels weak |
| 5. Opponent Design | Builds the force against the protagonist | No real opposition, protagonist is passive |
| 6. Narrative Engine | Assembles the full middle structure | Middle has events but no momentum |
| 7. Story Synthesis | Connects all elements into a working outline | Story "works on paper" but won't work on screen |

## Opponent Triangle

The protagonist needs an opponent that directly attacks the moral blind spot. Three positions:

**Main Opponent**: The character who most directly opposes the protagonist's conscious goal AND unconsciously threatens the blind spot. Often a mirror — they have the same blind spot but have acted on it further.

**Ally with Opposition**: A character who supports the protagonist's goal but challenges their worldview. Creates internal conflict without external antagonism.

**Apparent Ally (False Friend)**: A character who appears to support the protagonist but is actually reinforcing the blind spot. Their "help" makes things worse.

The opponent triangle is NOT about villain/hero — it's about which characters put pressure on the protagonist's moral wound from different angles.

## Diagnosing a Mushy Middle with Lyons

When a story has no center:

1. **Is there a stated Moral Blind Spot?** If not, start there. What does the protagonist falsely believe?
2. **Is the Immoral Effect visible?** If the audience can't SEE the harm the protagonist causes, the middle has no moral stakes.
3. **Does the opponent attack the blind spot?** If the main opponent is just a physical obstacle (not a moral one), the middle will feel like events, not story.
4. **Are there escalating pinches?** The protagonist's hot buttons must be triggered with increasing severity through the middle — first a pinch (minor), then a crunch (major).

If the answer to any of these is no, that is the structural hole. Route to the **lyons-story-engine** skill for a full BUILD or DIAGNOSE pass.

## Quick Reference: Moral Component Formula

```
BLIND SPOT:   [Protagonist] believes [false belief about the world/people].
IMMORAL EFFECT: Because of this, [protagonist] does [specific harmful behavior] to [specific characters].
MORAL TENSION: The middle will be driven by [escalating cost of maintaining the blind spot].
```

**Example:**
- Blind Spot: He believes that protecting his family means controlling every variable.
- Immoral Effect: He systematically dismantles his daughter's autonomy, her relationships, her career choices — all framed as love.
- Moral Tension: The more he controls, the more he destroys what he's trying to protect, until she has to choose between her life and him.
